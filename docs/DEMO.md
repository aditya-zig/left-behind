# Demo and submission handoff

## A three-minute walkthrough

1. **0:00–0:20 — Why this exists.** “A machine can stop transmitting without its story ending. Left Behind connects the hardware that stayed to the knowledge that came home.” Show the Moon hero. State that the visual material is NASA-published, not generated.
2. **0:20–0:40 — Begin.** Enter the four-chapter opening. Point out pause and skip; skip to the Moon for a short presentation.
3. **0:40–1:10 — Moon.** Inspect the NASA lunar-module model. Explain that its upper stage departed; only the descent stage remains. Open Facts & sources and the actual challenge-listed NSSDCA citation. Switch to the reflector to show why “left behind” does not always mean “dead.”
4. **1:10–1:50 — Mars.** Switch to Opportunity. Separate last contact from mission closure. Inspect Ingenuity's actual NASA model: five planned flights became seventy-two. Show that all narration is readable and sound is optional.
5. **1:50–2:15 — Original resources.** Inspect the NASA landing-site STL terrain, explicitly a reference rather than a georeferenced reconstruction. Open Sources and its per-file provenance register.
6. **2:15–2:40 — Beyond / legacy.** Search the collection, show Kepler in its separate deep-space category, then open The legacy for the Moon/Mars comparison and reflection prompt.
7. **2:40–3:00 — Close.** “We used NASA's mission records to establish each story, and NASA's models and images to show the machines without inventing their appearance. The archive makes the scientific evidence inspectable rather than hiding it behind a cinematic effect.”

## Suggested project description

Left Behind is an interactive, source-led documentary about NASA equipment that remains on the Moon and Mars. A guided ten-story journey and an eighteen-record archive connect engineering purpose, why hardware stayed, its documented status and its scientific legacy. Visitors can inspect original NASA reference models and landing-site terrain, compare Moon and Mars, and open claim-specific NASA sources. A separate deep-space collection prevents surface and orbital histories being conflated. Published NASA imagery, local asset provenance, readable transcripts and explicit uncertainty keep the experience grounded in evidence.

## Before an actual submission

- Confirm the event year, challenge availability, eligibility, team registration and final submission rules in the official event portal. The linked brief is labeled 2026; this repository makes no eligibility claim.
- Add the actual team name, contributors and contact details. Do not invent names or user-test results.
- Supply the production URL and GitHub URL; record a real demo using the running site.
- Review original NASA/contributor media terms and preserve credits. Explain concrete source usage, not merely link volume.
- Disclose AI assistance as described in About / README. No generated visual media is included.
- Check the deployed app on your own phone, with keyboard navigation and, if possible, a screen reader. Automated testing is not a substitute for this.
- If replacing optional device speech with recorded narration, obtain proper voice rights, agree a voice/reference and add attribution and audio QA. Do not imply astronaut endorsement.

This is handoff guidance, not a completed submission or a guarantee of judging results.
